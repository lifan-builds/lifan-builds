TOP 10 · October 31 announcement UTC; technical post dated October 2026

Anthropic’s automated researchers find harmful training effects behind harmless examples

Parallel Claude agents generate up to 5,000 training examples, test them against an accessible monitor, fine-tune a research model and share experiment logs. Fiction and roleplay pass screening yet teach behavior that transfers into the assistant’s own persona; reported results persist after flagged examples are removed, with reproductions on other checkpoints.

TOP 10 · October 29 report; October 30 Lambert analysis

Epoch’s Firefox experiments expose the cost of publishing fixes before shipping

GLM-5.3, released in August, compromised six September–October Firefox releases using information available at each release, combining public fixes with an unpublished vulnerability. Roughly $1,000 per version is marginal API cost after over $100,000 of Firefox research; comparable Chrome attempts failed despite another approximately $100,000.

Arca explains how persistent agents coordinate work across ten-day handoffs

Aman Singh describes work that pauses for people and decisions over as many as ten days, supported by per-client memory tracking responsibilities, completed actions and next steps. Arca routes models through internal benchmarks, keeps trading and money movement with human advisors, and translates generated data-query code into a constrained representation before backend execution.

Spawn’s founder details permanent agents, rapid recovery and expensive production feedback

Jacob Sansbury reports ten staff, $3.1 million in monthly tokens and 77,000 substantial changes over the preceding 30 days—not changes made this week. TOP 10 · October 30 announcements and Internet-Draft

Major AI providers back one training-preference signal with staggered rollout

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back a shared robots.txt policy token, `__usage_ai-training`, reducing the need to express training preferences separately for every provider. The draft separates content use from crawling: wildcard groups do not express this preference, and the token alone does not block retrieval.

TOP 10 · October 29, 07:48 UTC submission; newly surfaced this run

Panchenko credits ChatGPT with a proof he checked and rewrote

Dmitry Panchenko’s Mézard–Parisi preprint claims a matching lower bound for diluted spin-glass and random K-SAT free energy, removing a finite-overlap-support assumption. His disclosure says ChatGPT Astra found the argument after being asked for a new perturbation and simpler induction; he read, checked and rewrote it, with AI assistance, and distinguishes a separate OpenAI proof.

UK regulator investigates Meta’s proposed rights to publishers’ website content
