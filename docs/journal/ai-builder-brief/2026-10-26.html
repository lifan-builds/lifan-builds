OpenAI launches GPT-6.1 Terra to cut the cost of computer use

Terra is available through the API, ChatGPT Work and Codex, with standard prices of $1 input, $0.05 cached input and $5 output per million tokens; tool calling requires Responses. OpenAI reports a 15-point Agents’ Last Exam improvement over GPT-5.6 Terra at medium effort and about one-third the task cost.

LangChain shows how company agents separate identity, memory, and authority

Harrison Chase and Caspar Broekhuizen explain implementation choices across three internal agents: OpenSWE uses the caller’s identity for requests and its own app identity for background reviews; email tools are available only in direct messages, while company posting requires an allowlist. Shared and personal memory, sandbox credential injection, and scheduled runs complete the design.

Microsoft releases Agensh code so builders can test decentralized agent teams

The August-created repository now supplies the harness, launch scripts and reproduction guide: peers claim work, exchange findings and merge through Gitea, Mattermost and shared context without a central planner. Across five hard ProgramBench tasks, the authors report mean test-pass rates of 19.31% with one agent and 28.78% with 128 under six-hour budgets; the 1,024-agent result concerns Pandoc specifically.

Nanbeige releases Apache-licensed 33B weights for coding and office agents

Nanbeige4.3-33B is a dense, text-only model with 512K context and a configurable tool-use template, positioned as the teacher for future smaller agents. Its own matched-harness table reports 80.9 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 versus Qwen3.8-27B’s 73.0, but trails Qwen on SWE-Bench Pro, 60.3 versus 61.7.

Noise contributors improve forward-only training by recovering cross-token credit

The October 12-created speedrun repository now incorporates community experiments on 30M training tokens, including a forward readout that measures how a token’s changed keys and values affect later tokens. One fixed-model submission reports loss 3.3385 in 56 minutes on eight H100s; its own better-matched backpropagation baseline with weight averaging reaches 3.330, preventing a clean “backprop beaten” claim.

TOP 10 · October 25, 2026, 21:00 UTC original analysis

Max Woolf explains why cache pricing changes agent economics

Woolf’s new synthesis compares months of model prices, cache-read discounts and competing inference endpoints, arguing that open-weight providers constrain closed-model pricing. The useful mechanism is workload composition: repeated cached context can dominate token volume, making an input/output price comparison misleading even when both quoted rates are correct.
