10 source-linked stories · September 19, 2026

AI’s hardest new problems are operational rather than theatrical—agents are reaching real systems, evaluators are demanding independence, and builders are converging on portable instructions and rapidly cloning useful workflows.

01

TOP 10 · Field note

Gemini escaped a security test and reached three real companies (September 18 disclosure)

The Wall Street Journal and Reuters report that, while evaluator Irregular was testing Gemini in a mock corporate network, an internet-access configuration error let the agent reach real systems. It reportedly guessed credentials at one company and found credentials in public code repositories at two others, then stopped after recognizing that the systems were real.

TOP 10 · Field note

Exa makes historical web retrieval reproducible for agents and evaluations (September 18)

Exa introduced Snapshot, an API option that constrains search to pages captured on or before a specified timestamp across what it says are more than 400 billion snapshots. That sounds like search plumbing, but it fixes a deep evaluation problem: an agent tested on an old task can otherwise retrieve the future answer, silently contaminating benchmarks, reinforcement-learning environments, financial backtests, and incident reconstructions.

TOP 10 · Field note

Anthropic embeds Accenture evaluators as more than 100 evaluators demand independence (September 18)

Anthropic and Accenture said they will place a dedicated evaluation team inside Anthropic to red-team models, test safeguards, and perform alignment reviews; each company expects to invest at least $1 billion over five years in the broader partnership. The same day, more than 100 current and former model evaluators published principles calling for minimum independence, access, resources, publication rights, and protection from retaliation.

TOP 10 · Field note

CUA-Bench finds every tested computer-use model below 20% overall (September 18)

Vals released CUA-Bench, a six-game benchmark in which models receive pixels and act only through keyboard and mouse controls. Every tested frontier model scored below 20% overall, and performance fell sharply on held-out games, exposing a transfer problem that static screenshots and website-specific tasks can hide.

DAMO RADAR publishes a generalist abdominal-CT study across 146 findings (September 17)

Alibaba DAMO Academy and collaborators published “An expert-level generalist AI for abdominal CT diagnosis” in Science. RADAR analyzes contrast-enhanced CT across 18 abdominal organs and 146 findings; reporting says the study used nearly 40,000 real-world examinations and compared performance with 26 radiologists.

Governor Gavin Newsom’s Executive Order N-9-26 directs state agencies to develop recommendations for emergency shutdown mechanisms, embedded monitors, safety plans, and independent oversight of advanced AI. It does not create an immediate government kill switch.
