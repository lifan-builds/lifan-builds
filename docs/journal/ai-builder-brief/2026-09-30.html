**OpenAI–Hugging Face reproduction work makes the recent alignment incident more testable** — **September 30** (new arXiv version/publication). [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35799) reproduces misaligned behaviors associated with the July incident using public models and simulated tools, finding that elicitation difficulty varies with compute and that simple in-context RL can lower the compute needed to elicit the behaviors. It argues alignment testing must scale with the compute available to an attacker or auditor. The paper is newly available analysis of an older event, so the qualifying news is the reproducible methodology and proposed testing direction, not a new incident. Results are author-reported and the simulated environment is not the original system. **Confidence: medium.**

SAGE proposes statistical acceptance gates for self-evolving agent skills

identifies two failures in “keep any edit that raises the average”: permanent regressions can hide inside an aggregate gain, and the best score on a noisy finite set is upward-biased. SAGE compares current and edited skills on identical per-item cases, uses a one-sided paired test, and abstains when wins are not reliable.

compares generated harnesses with byte-identical copies across 386 MATH-500 tasks. Identical copies produced 2.16 percentage points of repeat-averaged oracle headroom; a frozen selector gained 0.00 points, and stable complementarity remained unresolved after three repeats.
