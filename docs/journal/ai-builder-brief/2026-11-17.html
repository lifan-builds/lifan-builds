TOP 10 · November 16, 2026, 16:10 UTC paper

Benchleaking finds memorized test answers that reverse open-model rankings

Across four base models and 20 benchmarks, few-shot continuations reproduce complete test questions and answers absent from their inputs; ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base reproduces 96.3% of HumanEval and 85.4% of OpenBookQA under strict matching. Giving detected leaked examples zero credit, with denominators unchanged, lowers its suite average from 85.17 to 68.55 and reverses its position among the four models.

OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 with cheaper Astra tokens and disclosed regressions

Astra and Sol are available through the API, ChatGPT Work and Codex on eligible paid plans; Astra costs $8 input/$0.40 cached/$40 output per million tokens, while Sol costs $2/$0.10/$10. TOP 10 · November 16, 2026 engineering retrospective

Claude Code’s team protects verification rules from the patches they judge

Addy Osmani explains an implemented workflow: app-running verification skills, 39 reliability rules triggered selectively by diffs, reviews bound to specific commits, and review instructions read from the base branch. Historical incidents become evaluation cases, stable merged changes supply controls, and tuning examples remain separate from the fixed test set.

TOP 10 · November 16, 2026 publication; June–October experiments

Together’s evaluation retrospective explains why unified skills initially made agents worse

Consolidating 14 skills initially reduced completion from 90.3% to 75% and approximately doubled cost; the router alone was 14,000 characters, encouraged excessive reading, and pointed agents toward stale examples. Cutting it below 6,000 characters, adding selective reference menus, updating examples and repairing misleading API errors brought completion to 70/72, or 97.2%, at roughly the original cost.

TOP 10 · November 15, 2026, 19:39 UTC paper

SWE-CoWork finds agents discover requirements but still fail complete handoffs

The benchmark places 101 real repositories inside simulated organizations with 26 rule-governed colleagues and evaluates requirements scattered across conversations, records and deferred replies. Astra passes 62.91% of fail-to-pass test nodes but completes only 7.92% of assignments; providing complete requirements raises partial performance to 78.84% while complete success stays at 7.92%.

Hugging Face moves language-model routing to OpenRouter and removes custom keys

Inference Providers now routes language-model requests through OpenRouter, retaining Hugging Face authentication and billing while selecting providers by price, speed and reliability. The consequential breaking change is that language models no longer accept users’ own provider keys; image and video custom keys remain supported.

TOP 10 · November 16, 2026 implementation analysis

Quail explains why hybrid-model reuse needs recurrent checkpoints alongside KV caches

Arnav Dhariya and Shreya Shankar show that reusing a document requires saving Gated DeltaNet state and convolution buffers together with attention KV, then restoring that checkpoint before each new question. Their memory manager evicts both together and prices retained state by expected recomputation saved per byte; fixed checkpoints dominate short documents but amortize over long ones.

Alibaba opens Qwen4-Max’s general API after its restricted preview

The private-weight model accepts text, images and video within a one-million-token context and supports structured outputs, tool calls and OpenAI/Anthropic-compatible interfaces. QwenCloud lists $1.50 input/$4.50 output per million tokens, with cached input at $0.075; its general API is the new event beyond the earlier Token Plan preview.

TOP 10 · November 16, 2026 announcement; effective January 1, 2027

Anthropic shortens high-severity vulnerability disclosure deadlines from ninety to thirty days

Newly reported high/critical issues move to a 30-day window and other issues to 45 days, with escalation for unresponsive maintainers after 15 days. Anthropic attributes the change to faster AI-assisted rediscovery and offers patches or mitigations where possible; the policy affects human-reviewed findings, excluding unreviewed OSS Scanner output.

TOP 10 · November 16, 2026 agreements

OpenAI’s embedded-assessor pilots expose both development evidence and access limits

Apollo Research, METR, Goodfire and Transluce will investigate alignment and safeguards during training and internal use; Apollo specifies six evaluators over four months. Apollo’s own disclosure makes the consequential distinction: this pilot does not yet meet its full access/disclosure criteria, because OpenAI can withhold sensitive in-scope material and redact supporting evidence, although unfavorable verdicts cannot be suppressed merely for being unfavorable.
