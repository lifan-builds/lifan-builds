TOP 10 · November 2 experiment and technical explanation

Bifocal tests expose long-reasoning failures hidden by short-answer attention benchmarks

Lukas’s new comparison distills four Qwen3-4B students on the same approximately 4.1 billion tokens while matching attention-state memory: Bifocal combines a 448-token window with an FP32 recurrent state costing another 128 cached tokens per layer. Sliding windows lead on short-answer tasks, but Bifocal solves 1.8 times as many of the 96 problems grouped as requiring long answers; the overall math average is only 15.5% versus 13.8%.

Meituan delivers LongCat-2.5 API access while weights remain unavailable

The 1.6-trillion-parameter model activates approximately 48 billion parameters per token and combines discrete visual tokens with longer agent trajectories, failure-derived training tasks and asynchronous reinforcement learning. AiSOC corrects model attribution and lowers an inflated alert-reduction result

Maintainers found 72 evaluation calls with no recorded serving-model identity because direct endpoints bypassed the gateway header their reader expected. TOP 10 · November 1 original guest essay on Terence Tao’s blog

Calegari proposes changing mathematical rewards as AI makes proofs cheaper

Frank Calegari argues that producing a theorem and understanding its significance are separating, and offers concrete institutional changes: assess all work as potentially AI-assisted, emphasize direct demonstrations of understanding in hiring, demand explanatory journal papers, and broaden graduate education while retaining technical skills. His example is using AI to explore connections between arithmetic and hyperbolic geometry whose prerequisite assembly previously made investigation prohibitive.
