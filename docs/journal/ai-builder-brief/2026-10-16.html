A new memory synthesis explains why agents retain obsolete beliefs

Rahul Vimalkanth and Tanish Chudiwal separate memory storage, update rules, and read rules, then derive why additive associative memory mixes conflicting values instead of replacing an obsolete association. Their changed-function-signature example connects the mathematics to coding agents: retaining both API signatures does not establish which one governs the next call.

Reflection launches Apache-licensed Beam with a dedicated coding-agent harness

Reflection’s first model is a text-only 501B-total, 23B-active MoE with a 1M-token context, BF16/FP8/NVFP4 checkpoints, and day-one Transformers, vLLM, and SGLang support. The release includes a Rust-based harness, structured response parsing, preserved reasoning across turns, and post-training recipes, giving builders an unusually complete starting point for evaluating a model together with its runtime.

NVIDIA compresses Nemotron agents into a 24GB local deployment target

The Nemotron 3.5 family combines Super VL’s 120B multimodal model with Bolt 74B-A9B, compressed and mostly quantized to about two bits to fit a 24GB target. Bolt’s technical write-up explains structured expert compression, quantization-aware distillation, and LiLiCorr speculative decoding; NVIDIA reports about 84 tokens/second on its specified RTX Spark benchmark.

StepFun delivers Step 5 Preview weights after September’s API launch

StepFun’s newly downloadable BF16 and FP8 artifacts turn a previously announced model into a self-hosting option: roughly 600B total parameters, 27B active, and up to 1M tokens of context. TOP 10 · October 15, 2026

Mistral ships CPU-friendly Timestral models for probabilistic multivariate forecasting

Timestral spans 5M–209M parameters, produces 21 forecast quantiles, and supports past-only, categorical, and known-future covariates. Its design separates forecast tokens from historical observations and restricts covariate attention to avoid exposing future targets; Mistral reports leading zero-shot results on GIFT-Eval, TIME, and fev-bench, with 10–140ms CPU inference in one specified scenario.
