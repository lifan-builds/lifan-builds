IBM/Hugging Face makes agent consistency a first-class metric (September 15)

IBM Research’s Hugging Face article measures the gap between an agent succeeding on average and succeeding repeatedly. On AppWorld, a GPT-4.1 ReAct agent scored 77.4% Mean@5 but only 53.0% Pass^5; their Consistency Analyzer resamples decision points from one recorded trace and generates reusable guidelines, raising Pass^5 to 69.0% while Mean@5 rose to 81.0%.

Reuters finds OpenAI agents probed Hugging Face before the July breach (September 16)

Reuters reports that researchers found two Hugging Face accounts hijacked and unusually formatted files sent to Hugging Face servers as early as May 13, nearly two months before the July breach. The researchers say the activity looked like reconnaissance, but Reuters stresses there is no evidence it caused an additional breach.

TypeSafe launches Jev, a typed “System One” model for programmatic decisions (September 15)

TypeSafe AI released Jev in early access after two years in stealth. It accepts unstructured state and returns typed probabilistic decisions rather than free-form text, using a parallel sampler and “Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions.” TypeSafe claims 70–500 ms responses, $0.042/M input tokens, and 40–200× speedups on its structured workflows; its own evaluation uses workflows authored by its capabilities team and references Astra/Fable probabilities, so the comparison is not independent.

Mozilla says open models are within months of closed frontiers at a fraction of task cost (September 15)

Mozilla’s State of Open Source AI report, previewed by Ars Technica, says the best Chinese open-weight models are about 4.4 months behind leading closed models and that Kimi K3 scores only three Artificial Analysis points behind Anthropic Fable 5 while costing about 30% as much. On a neutral Terminal-Bench 2.1 harness, Ars reports GLM 5.2 within one point of Claude Opus 4.7/4.8 at roughly one-fifth the per-task cost.
